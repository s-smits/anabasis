
# Task difficulty with a fixed product

Formerly the `task-difficulty-and-climb` skill; `run-climb-lab` owns it.

Find harder decisions the existing Built Harness can attempt and the verifier can
distinguish. More rows, longer text or a higher level label do not establish that.
The Builder authors the task families and their meaning; the controller owns the
condition, admission, measurement and next-move rules.

## Start from the measured capability

Read the exact opening source, accepted bundle and case rows. State what the
verifier actually checks, including declared omissions. A firmware battery may
prove cross-compilation, pin allocation and configuration arithmetic while excluding
runtime peripheral behaviour. Its score supports the former scope only.

Keep verified, unaccepted and non-result denominators separate. A 25/25 verified
battery is a capability result on those tasks and evidence that no failure boundary
was located there. It does not prove arbitrary-domain competence. An all-fail
battery also needs its accepted submissions and failure ownership read before a
limit claim. No verified cases means no difficulty evidence; inspect the existing
rebuild or environment route instead of treating absence as a zero score.

Difficulty has its own denominator: once a battery has a truth-verified case,
unaccepted attempts remain failures in its difficulty evidence. Read the owning
evidence reader and frozen thresholds for the exact bands, intervals and classifications.
Do not copy historical numerical examples or use a point pass rate in place of the
recorded interval. These statistics do not prescribe the default Builder's next experiment.
Preserve the identities that make its history comparable.

## Make the task require a decision

For the selected family, identify:

- the domain artifact the solver must produce and the judgement it must exercise;
- the public validity relation, data and installed capabilities needed to solve it;
- plausible wrong artifacts each material check must reject, and equivalent correct
  artifacts it must accept;
- the axis expected to make the decision harder and its realised value in task bytes.

Moving a published magnitude is the cheapest thing an author can do and the easiest to mistake for
a climb. Read the check-tier histogram and the edge verdict that
`.claude/skills/whole-run-investigation/scripts/climb-velocity.mjs` produces before writing that a
battery got harder: only `escalated` changes what a solver has to reason about, and a histogram that
did not move says a battery did not either, however many numbers or scenarios did.

Useful axes include interacting constraints, resource limits, dependency depth,
irrelevant candidates and cross-source information. Choose the few that express
the capability. Domain meaning remains model-owned; structural validation cannot
prove that a longer array or larger number is semantically harder.

Give the solver requirements and public facts, while withholding a sufficient
construction algorithm across everything the agent can read. A tool that evaluates a
proposed design may be legitimate engineering support. Judge its actual returns
and use before calling it an answer recipe or removing it. Many possible artifacts
disprove neither a universal recipe nor an easy shortcut.

## Prove the frozen representation admits the move

Read `src/run/next-move.ts` and the candidate admission path before proposing a move. Trace the
actual caller and its return validator. There is one authoring path: the separate fixed-harness
session that prescribed a selected level, family composition and parent lineage through
`DIFFICULTY.json` is gone, and with it `src/author/climb-transition.ts` and its extra restrictions.
Anything asking for a declared parent bijection is reading a mechanism that has left the source.

Check the move against the adopted predecessor, which may differ from the latest
held candidate. A task-only scope establishes unchanged agent and correctness-model
bytes and a valid new battery. Controls belong to the new tasks;
freezing verifier identity does not mean reusing controls bound to obsolete tasks.

Check coupled representations early. If a public value is duplicated inside an
opaque JSON string, a one-path move may break agreement. An explicit single-axis contract must
account for every changed copy; a default task redesign may change both. Enumerate the relevant
numeric and array inputs and attempt a
falsifier at the smallest real layer. Do not call the task impossible merely
because one proposal failed, and do not weaken the comparison to admit it.

A stubbed external tool can isolate a structural contradiction. Label that scope
and retain the exact snapshot, prediction, stub and result. It proves no compiler,
solver or live behaviour. Keep protected verifier details out of author feedback;
fix the shared authoring contract or existing route instead of coaching a candidate.

## Follow the decision through to measurement

Distinguish these receipts:

`selected climb → delivered author context → candidate → admission → measured cases → promotion`

A projected harder level may lose to blocking review feedback. A changed task hash
may fail admission. A measured candidate may be held and leave the previous harness
adopted. Report the furthest branch actually reached and the owner of each refusal.
Read the typed promotion record; a file count or log line does not prove promotion.

A climb changes task difficulty on a preserved agent/verifier condition. A rebuild
can change that condition, so its score movement has different attribution. Verify
bundle and task hashes, model pins, tool identity and recorded decision together.
Do not reconstruct the current architecture from old repair labels or PR prose.

For repeated authoring failures, inspect the existing identity, repetition bound,
restart replay and outer retry route together. Post-restore Git status cannot prove
the author made no edits. A bounded inner terminal must not open an unbounded outer
retry. Changed diagnoses and gate settlements must receive the reset their owner
defines. An authoring refusal establishes no solver limit.

## Run and assess

Use the parent `run-climb-lab` skill to freeze one attributable question and prediction. Use
`run-improvement-campaign` and `launch-run` for already-authorised paid work; this
Skill creates no launch, retry or stopping authority. When the operator asks for
real Built Harness runs initiated by Anabasis, follow Anabasis's measured cases and subsequent
decisions. Diagnostic simulations remain separate supporting evidence.

A redesigned battery may have no one-to-one comparison with its predecessor, which must remain
explicit: ids change and nothing maps them back.
Compare the realised public inputs, then split outcomes into changed
and unchanged tasks and report each family's denominator. Same-family cases can
require different answers: compare each against its own public facts before using
a passing case as a contrast. Inspect recorded prompt digests where available.
A newly failed unchanged task is repeat-condition evidence, not proof that the
authored move made it harder. Keep this attribution separate from the controller's
full-battery difficulty decision.

Separate semantic discrimination from observed solver difficulty. Moving a bound
can expose a plausible wrong algorithm while every changed task still passes.
A public-data falsifier may establish that new distinction; only the Anabasis battery
establishes how its Built Harness performed. Flat scores or tool-call counts prove
neither a useless axis nor increased difficulty. Cross-compiling target firmware
and executing a separate host model proves their recorded scopes; a claim about
the target's runtime behaviour needs evidence connecting those two artifacts.
Report the realised axis, preserved and changed identities, admission/promotion,
prediction result, omissions and unresolved cause.
Check whether saturation recurs on the same frozen harness or follows a rebuild.
Read the actual next-move policy before calling either a stable limit. The default Builder chooses
its next experiment; historical saturation and infeasible-hold rules do not impose its stop.
A fresh claim or an admitted climb alone proves no improvement.
