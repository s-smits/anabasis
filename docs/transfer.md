# Transfer ledger: v1 to v2

This ledger tracks each part of v1's loop: what it did, and whether v2 has it. v1 here is harness-builder-v4 at `7af7315`, the private repository v1 was built in; this repository holds v1 as a squashed history whose last tree is `5bc9988`, so `git show 5bc9988:<path>` reads most of the files below, line numbers are `7af7315`'s, and a few paths (such as `src/backends/claude-backend.ts`) were never published here. Paths below sit under v1's `src/` unless they start with `tools/`, `vendor/`, `starters/` or `packages/`. `plan:<line>` cites the plan of record, "Anabasis one plan".

Each session walks v1's loop and builds the gap that most delays a live end-to-end round. It proves that gap with faux tests, then with a cheap live run, and lands it as a stacked PR. Then the session updates the row here.

Statuses:
- **ported**: copied or sed from v1, cited at the copy.
- **replaced**: a v2 mechanism does the job differently.
- **dropped**: cut on purpose, with the plan line that cuts it.
- **missing**: needed and not built.
- **unsure**: the plan doesn't settle it.
- **deferred**: behind a plan gate, and not built before it. Promotion and the controller loop wait on gate G5, a step against the reference harness, which is the Starter Built Harness until a step is promoted (`docs/plan.md`, Phases 5 and 6).

Two facts frame the rest:
- **v1 has no "rollback".** The word never occurs. A rollback is a promotion decision of "held", recorded as `recordProductDecision({adopt:false})`, which keeps the product selected before.
- **v1 has no head-to-head comparison.** v1 measures each candidate on its own Builder-written battery and promotes it by clauses: unmeasured, claim-refused, zero-verified, unbound task set or evaluator, stale identity, fingerprint drift.

A row marked **open #N** is built in pull request #N. #2 to #15 are on main since 8 October (`b26e531`), so a row that names one of them is on main; #17 and later are the open stack above it.

### What those campaigns left in the Mac's temp folders

Read on 10 October, when the suite at `41e0533` (open #139) failed one test on the Mac on a 60-second limit. Two findings, both of the macOS command wall. The Linux wall has neither: it gives each command an empty `/tmp` and its own temp folder (`src/verify/solve-command-isolation.ts`).

- **A command's Python and make wrote outside the command's own temp folder.** Apple's launcher for `/usr/bin/python3`, `make`, `git` and `cc` hands the tool the user's shared temp folder whatever `TMPDIR` the command was given. By 10 October the solves and sessions on the Mac had left 1,526,961 entries there and 34,610 in `/tmp`, about 121 GB in 5.6 million files, every one a folder or a file that a script had made for one PHREEQC run. They lay outside every solve's disk cap, and every `bun` started under that folder took 3.6 s where it takes 0.07 s, which is what failed the test. Mended in open #143: a command's search path leads with the developer directory's own tools, which keep `TMPDIR`, and `SDKROOT` is set so that the compiler runs without the launcher. In the first ten minutes of two Builder sessions on that source the user's temp folder gained no entry. The leftovers were moved out by hand.
- **`/tmp` is one folder for every solve, session and campaign.** The wall grants it because build scripts and models spell it literally, and its comment names the limit. A campaign opened at `ae66f1d` on 10 October shows what it costs: within its first minute the buffer Builder made `/tmp/pq`, the name the Builder of the campaign before had chosen, and found that session's work there, a built PHREEQC and its answers to two practice tasks. No held-out task was in it. `/tmp` also held files that comparison solves of the campaigns above had written while they worked on held-out tasks, under names such as `/tmp/s.py`, where a later Builder could read them. The 203 entries those campaigns' logs name were moved out by hand at 12:34Z, except those the two running sessions had touched. Entries under generated names remain.

What the two logs above hold of it. Six buffer solve commands listed a shared temp root, and two got other commands' folder names back; one of the two took the newest folder for its own and failed on it, and neither read a file in one. No solve named a folder a Builder had made. At most 12 of buffer's 179 solves, and none of recode's 126, named a `/tmp` entry that another solve also named while both ran (`x`, `s.json`, `m.log`, and a `cd /tmp`): nine on the Starter, of which four were rejected, and three on a new harness. Nothing in the log says whether one read the other's bytes. The readings in the table stand with that bound.

Not mended: `/tmp` on macOS. Denying each entry that was there before a solve began costs a command 0.5 s at 860 entries and grows with the square of the count. Refusing `/tmp` and leaving `TMPDIR` is the Darwin equal of the Linux wall's empty `/tmp`, and it changes what a solver meets, so it is the operator's.

## 2. Builder session

The Builder is `src/builder/session.ts`, `tools.ts` and `prompt.ts` (open #5, #13). `build()` runs one session from a parent harness state, which is a Built Harness. It copies the parent whole into the Builder's home as `harness/`. ### The Starter rewrite of 9 October

Until then a v2 solver was v1's Built shell: `bash` with v1's description, v1's six starter tools and an artifact-writer, under Claude Code's prompt with a paragraph and a tool roster appended. It is now pi's coding agent as pi releases it, with v2's choices at four seams: the command wall behind every tool's operations, the answer as a file, the duty and the wall's facts in pi's own prompt slots, and the harness's guide as pi's project context. What decided each change was read from the solves on record: 209 finished solves in six campaigns of 8 and 9 October (buffer, truss, recode and the pr55 pilot), 62 of them under the Starter at a two-hour wall.

Three more things the same logs showed are not changed by the rewrite, and bear on the budget:

- The wall sized nothing. At two hours no Starter solve was ended by it, and a solve submitted at a median of 4% (buffer), 9% (recode), 19% (truss) and 30% (pilot) of it. At five minutes the median solve submitted after 93 s, and 27 of 46 were rejected.
- On truss and recode 69% and 87% of a median solve's seconds were inside commands, and commands of a minute or more held 89% and 96% of that. The command limit is the limit a solver meets: it named a timeout in a quarter to a half of its calls, and half the truss solves had a command at or past the default.
- Every two-hour Starter solve installed packages for itself (62 of 62), into homes of 40 to 200 MB.

Two holes in the budget's reading were closed with the rewrite. A command cut at its limit ends with its CPU unmeasured, and a comparison read the partial sum as the whole: a task with such a valid solve was first left out of a CPU budget's reading and counted as unread, and since the fix plan's row F14 no budget is given in CPU seconds at all: a budget names the model's turns or the attempt's seconds, and CPU is a reported figure (`src/kernel/compare.ts`). The rewrite is a new condition. The Starter has another digest and every solver reads another prompt, so no earlier solve counts for it, and it needs its own Starter solves and its own reading of one harness against itself before anything is compared (`docs/plan.md`).
