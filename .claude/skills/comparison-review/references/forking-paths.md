# How a review deceives itself

A comparison's terms are fixed before its first solve (rule 4). A review after it has no such
protection unless it builds its own, and it can take a hundred cuts of a small result without one
false statement. Each entry names the failure and what stops it. Nothing here makes a review
confirmatory. It keeps exploration honest, so that what leaves it is a lead of known strength.

## The record of looks

- **The hidden look.** A cut run, disliked and never mentioned. *Stop:* every cut has a ledger row
  before it runs, saying why. An unplanned cut is allowed and marked `after seeing`.
- **The uncounted family.** Ten scripts of cuts read as one investigation. *Stop:* report the number
  of cuts beside any pattern ("one of 14 cuts"). On 5 November nobody could give that number.
- **Rewriting history.** A row reworded once its answer is known. *Stop:* the ledger only grows.
  Correct by a new row that names the old one.
- **Pretending to be blind.** A review always follows a result. *Stop:* the first thing the ledger
  holds is what its author has already seen.

## Data used twice

- **Confirming on the pattern's own data.** *Stop:* the seal. The sealed half is read once, against
  rows written before, in a block of its own.
- **A seal too small to confirm.** Four sealed tasks cannot read a step. *Stop:* say so. The seal
  still falsifies a story that predicts a direction, and confirmation comes from a fresh test.
- **Burned tasks reused as evidence.** A proposal drawn from reading a task is tested on that task and
  passes. *Stop:* keep a burned list: every task whose deliverable, trace or rubric was read closely.
  A burned task may check for regressions and never counts for the proposal it suggested. Reviewers
  burn tasks too, so count what each session read. When the benchmark runs out of unburned tasks, it
  has stopped being held out for this system, and the report says so.
- **The benchmark taught by degrees.** Each round's small fix fits the benchmark a little more. *Stop:*
  each fix passes the gate on its own, and the burned list carries across reviews.
- **A proposal tested on its own tasks.** On 5 November both designs for the next run, this skill's
  author's and the blind reviewer's, repeated the same 8 tasks with an arm whose changes came from
  reading them. *Stop:* those tasks may still measure noise, which no proposal suggested. The arm drawn
  from them runs on tasks nobody has read.

## Choosing what to read

- **Reading only the losses.** Every explanation is confirmed by the rows chosen for it. *Stop:* read
  wins and losses with one procedure, and require the favoured story to predict both. A story that
  explains the 8 rubrics lost and not the 3 won is half a story.
- **Selecting on extremes.** The worst task is partly bad luck, and it will look better next time
  whatever changes. *Stop:* read every task of the half, or a draw fixed by the hash.
- **The garden of measures.** Points read a loss, so criteria are tried, then rubrics. *Stop:* the
  ledger freezes the primary measure, and any other is reported beside it, as description, always.
  On 5 November the operator was told p 0.25 on criteria, while the benchmark's own measure of points
  read p 0.074 on the same eight tasks. No one meant to pick the milder figure, which is why the
  freeze has to come first.
- **Subgroups after the fact.** "It lost only on the social science tasks." *Stop:* a subgroup is a
  cut, counted, and with eight tasks it is an anecdote.

## Explaining

- **The just-so story.** A mechanism that fits and predicts nothing. *Stop:* rival explanations, one
  innocent and one blaming the instrument, each with a count that would falsify it.
- **The mechanism with no trace.** "It stopped early because the guide said 20 turns", where no text
  cites it and most rollouts went past 20. *Stop:* name the event or trace that joins cause to effect,
  or call it unproved. A false statement can be a defect without being the cause, and the report keeps
  the two apart.
- **Averages across arms' own recoveries.** Fewer turns read as less effort when part of the gap is the
  other arm repairing its environment. *Stop:* sort before comparing (level 9).
- **The sighted card writer.** A blind session counts the shapes, but an author who knows the direction
  chose them, so the blinding starts one step too late. On 5 November most cards came from what had
  been lost. *Stop:* write a card from every kind of row, won and lost, count the two in the report,
  and let a reader who has not seen the result answer the mirror question.
- **Instrument taken for ability.** A grader that never read the page. *Stop:* level 6 before level 8.
- **Claims of absence.** "Nothing used it", without the search. *Stop:* the symbol, its callers and a
  check in the same turn (`AGENTS.md`, section 9).

## Statistics

- **"Within the noise" with no noise measured.** *Stop:* quote a clean A/A or say none exists. A swing
  between two conditions is no A/A.
- **Mixed conditions read as a comparison.** *Stop:* solves apart are a description (rule 5; section 9).
- **A p-value from discovery.** Read as evidence although the cut was chosen on the data. *Stop:*
  discovery reports counts and effect sizes, and a p only on the primary measure the ledger froze.
- **Several tests, one level.** *Stop:* where several shapes are tested on one fresh set, hold them
  with Holm, or name one primary and report the rest as description. Fix which before the set is read.
- **Power ignored.** A lost comparison at eight tasks, read as "worse", or a tied one as "equal".
  *Stop:* give the test's size and what it could detect (`size.ts`). No step is no proof of equality.
- **The winner's curse.** The first size seen is inflated by being the one noticed. *Stop:* size a
  confirmation for half the effect seen.
- **Optional stopping.** More rollouts until it clears, or a stop when it does. *Stop:* fix rollouts
  before the first (rule 4). A top-up is another comparison, reported beside the first.
- **Rate without denominator.** *Stop:* every rate names its denominator and what it left out.

## People and sessions

- **The anchored reviewer.** Told the conclusion, it confirms it. *Stop:* pointers and open questions,
  no counts (the brief in `report.md`). The operator's request of 5 November, "only give it hints", is
  this rule. Tell it where to look and what could be worth checking, never what you found.
- **Blind in name only.** Paths and tool names can reveal an arm: `.toolchain/`, a guide's words, the
  folder's label. *Stop:* neutral names by hash, and say what could still leak.
- **Agreement as replication.** Three sessions reading one data set are one data set. *Stop:* count
  them as reads, and confirm only on fresh solves.
- **A session's report taken as fact.** *Stop:* recount it (section 11 of `AGENTS.md`). A confident
  number in a report is a lead.
- **Pressure to please.** A bad result invites a comforting frame, and a good one a triumphant frame.
  *Stop:* report the same counts in the same order either way, and correct your own earlier words
  plainly when the review refutes them.

## Controls that cost little

Run them while the data is on hand, and report them with the finding:

- **The mirror question.** Would this change be proposed if the result had gone the other way?
- **Label swap.** Swap the arms and run the same cut. A story of equal size for the swapped side says
  the cut finds stories.
- **Placebo tasks.** Apply the favoured measure to the tied tasks. A strong difference where the score
  did not move says the measure does not explain the score.
- **Leave one out.** Drop each task in turn, and see whether the sign or the story stands.
- **Shared-miss check.** A claimed weakness that the other arm shows just as much is no difference.

## What a review may honestly conclude

A review on one comparison can say: this level is broken; this statement is false; this much of the
gap sits here; these explanations survived and these fell; this shape recurs in N of M records. It
cannot say that a change will help, or that a cause is proved, until a fresh comparison reads it.
