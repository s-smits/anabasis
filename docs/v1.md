# What v1 recorded, and why it did not climb

9 October 2026. This is Phase 0's account of v1 for `docs/plan.md`. Until now v2 held two ledger
rows on the subject (`docs/transfer.md`).

v1 is this repository's history before v2 took it over on 11 October 2026; this account reads it at
commit `3f93d06`, as v2 copied from it there, and its last tree is `5bc9988`. Its Builder wrote the
harness, the tasks and the checks, and one controller measured the harness on the Builder's own
test sets, which v1 called batteries. v1 defined a climb step as four things in a row: a true fail,
meaning the solver's answer was wrong and the check was right to reject it; a change to the harness
in response; the same task then solved; and a harder battery the solver again fails in part. It
never completed one.

## Where each figure comes from

Two sources are kept apart below, because only one of them could be read for this account.

  four blockers to a climb", rule 10 of its hard rules, and "Reading the climb as the operator".

- **The operator's account:** his summary of 8 October of v1's notes

The two windows differ, so the totals do not agree and are not reconciled here.

## The five problems

| Problem | What v1 recorded |
| --- | --- |
| The test was bounded by its author's ability | Every admitted task carried a witness its Builder had solved or searched for, and in all 91 runs the solver was the Builder's model. The solver beat the Builder's own reference on 5 of 6 tasks in one run and on 107 of 134 in two truss runs. 265 of 299 batteries were passed in full *(operator)*. |
| Label noise in the test | When a task was failed, the check was usually at fault: 36 of 47 rehearsal misses came from a faulty check or instrument (v1 #124, as v2's ledger cites it), and 17 of 22 fails counted as limits were disputed *(operator)*. Of 322 batteries, 33 were partial and 11 held a fail nobody contested. |
| High variance between repeated solves | In one truss run, 13 tasks solved 59 times failed twice, and both fails passed in the battery beside them. v1 called such a fail a flip. |
| Harder tests became broader instead of deeper | When a probe at 2 of 6 or 3 of 6 was grown to 25 tasks, the added tasks passed in 10 of 13 recorded cases. v1 did not isolate the cause: 10 of the 11 former-limit tasks carried unchanged passed again, and 11 of the 13 transitions also changed the scoring program. 71 of 74 scored batteries after a widened edge passed whole. After a difficulty raise the median solve time was 1.07 times the previous one *(operator)*. |
| Failures were rarely acted on | In six chances after an uncontested fail, the Builder changed the harness once, dropped or eased the failing task three times, and twice the task passed unchanged. |

## What was tried

- **The one change that made tasks harder** was several requirements that share one limit. On the
  truss pack series of 15 September, one added interaction per task passed 22 of 23; the same
  requirements stacked inside one shared mass limit passed 7 of 20 and 2 of 23.
- **A stronger or longer-searching author did not bind.** A medium Builder over a low solver
  verified 139 passes and no fail. Six limits were reset at 1.02 times the best of longer searches,
  three of 45 minutes and three of 3.4 hours, and the solver passed 5 of the 6. v1 calls the effect
  measured on six tasks only. A Builder at higher effort than the solver gave 6 of 6, twice
  *(operator)*.
- **Choosing the answer first did not bind.** On 1 October six instances were built from a chosen
  answer at a size where every greedy and a 12,000-move local search failed. The solver passed all
  six, in 2.5 to 26 minutes of a 120-minute wall. v1's own explanation: "The published rules let the
  solver compute every check on its own candidate, so it wrote a search and ran it until the checks
  cleared."
- **Hiding the grading instrument did not bind.** Twelve firmware tasks re-solved without the
  simulator all passed, each in under three minutes; 8 of 12 submissions were the draft the solver
  had before its first simulation. A second such trial gave 20 of 21 *(operator)*.
- **Another model solving a model's tasks** gave 9 of 10, and rehearsal solves used as a filter
  passed 895 of 939 *(both operator)*.
- **A harness's own test flattered it.** One truss harness passed 75 of 75 tasks its Builder wrote,
  and the same agent under another solver passed 2 of 23 verified hard tasks of a shared pack.

## What was reliable, and what was never tested

The passes were reliable: on 23 September v1's Judge had vetoed 5 of 1,411 verifier passes.

Two things were left unmeasured. One is a task whose answer the field already recorded, such as a
merged fix or a published result. The other is a second author for the test: a split build, in
which another agent writes the correctness model behind a wall, was written on 30 September on the
branch `claude/answer-builder` and neither landed nor ran. v2's Examiner is that second author.

## What v2 takes from this

`docs/plan.md` draws eight principles from these results. The three that change code first:

1. How the solver does with a shell and nothing else is measured first, since v1's solver wrote
   its own search and passed: the first round compares the harness a Builder leaves with the
   Starter Built Harness it forked, and the starter's own result at several budgets says whether
   the tasks leave a harness anything to add.
2. A rejection is verified before it counts, since most of v1's were the check's.
3. Both harnesses get one compute budget, and a valid answer within it is what is compared, since
   v1's limit never bound and most batteries passed whole. No stronger solver proves a task
   possible, since the solver matched whatever the author's side found, and a measured objective
   is added only if validity leaves too many ties.
